Academic Methodology

Q-MolGen Platform Methodology

Theoretical basis, computational formulations, dataset provenance, and research limitations.

1. Cheminformatics Engine (RDKit)

Chemical validity is verified using RDKit's Kekulization and valency perception modules. SMILES strings are normalized into canonical representations, eliminating duplicate chemical formulations. Descriptors (MW, LogP, TPSA, HBD, HBA) are calculated using 2D topological graph invariants.

2. Quantum Machine Learning Formulation

Classical descriptors are normalized onto the interval $[0, \pi]$ and mapped into parameterized quantum states $|\Phi(x)\rangle$ using a $ZZ\text{FeatureMap}$ with linear qubit entanglement. Quantum kernel matrix elements $K_{ij} = |\langle\Phi(x_i)|\Phi(x_j)\rangle|^2$ compute non-linear inner products in a 16-dimensional Hilbert space ($N=4$ qubits) executed on the Qiskit Aer simulator.

3. Multi-Objective Optimization

Candidate fitness is evaluated via a composite objective balancing aqueous solubility targets, synthetic accessibility approximations, and penalty filters for compounds violating Lipinski's Rule of Five.

4. Academic Integrity & Limitations

All predictions represent computational approximations. No computational model replaces in vitro binding assays, pharmacokinetic profiling, or physical crystal structure validation.
